    How repeaters work¶

    This page explains the ideas behind the setup tool: what a repeater does, how scopes and regions decide where messages go, and what each command in the tool's list means. You don't need to read it first: the tool asks one question at a time and explains every command as you go.

    What a repeater is¶

    A repeater passes messages on so they reach further.

    It's a MeshCore radio that stays switched on in one place, ideally up high on a roof or a mast. Nobody chats on it. People chat on a companion: the small radio they pair with the MeshCore app on their phone or computer. When a repeater hears a message, it can send it out again, so it reaches people who are too far away to hear the companion. Each repeater that passes a message on is one hop.

    Before it passes a message on, a repeater checks two things:

    • Is the message labelled for somewhere it serves? Its region list decides that. See Scopes and regions.
    • Has it gone too far already? After 16 hops, no repeater passes it on.

    Every device nearby has to use the same radio settings, or they can't hear each other. Most of Canada uses the Canada radio settings; some local groups use their own.

    To set one up, use the setup tool. For hardware and where to put it, see repeater devices and mounting options.

    What a repeater observer is¶

    A repeater observer is a repeater that also reports what it hears to live.meshcore.ca, so everyone can see how the mesh is doing. It needs Wi-Fi to do that.

    That's why it needs an ESP32 board: nRF52 boards have no Wi-Fi. It runs the observer firmware from the observer flasher, under MQTT Observer Firmware. Its reports go over your Wi-Fi to the two MeshCore Canada servers, using MQTT (a common way for devices to send reports to a server).

    It works in one of two ways:

    • Repeat and observe: a full repeater that also reports what it hears. Most people pick this.
    • Listen only: it reports what it hears to live.meshcore.ca but never passes anything on.

    Either way it reports what it hears. Listen only is handy indoors or next to another repeater, where repeating would only add noise. You can switch later with set repeat on or set repeat off.

    A repeater observer is one kind of observer. Others use RemoteTerm, Home Assistant or a small computer next to a radio: Set up a network observer helps you choose one. Observer data and privacy explains what the reports contain.

    Scopes and regions¶

    A scope is a label on a message saying how far it should travel. A region list is the labels a repeater agrees to pass on.

    That's the whole idea. For example:

    • A message labelled ytr stays around Belleville. Repeaters in Quinte West have ytr on their list, so they pass it on. Kingston's repeaters don't, so it stops there.
    • A message labelled on crosses Ontario, because every Ontario repeater has on on its list.

    One code, two names¶

    The same short codes show up in two places:

    • On a message, the label is a scope. You set it on your companion and on each channel. The MeshCore app calls it a region scope.
    • On a repeater, the labels it passes on are its regions, kept in its region list.

    A region is also the area a repeater serves. Regions are named after the nearest airport code: Belleville is in ytr, Quinte West. A repeater's region decides which messages it passes on.

    Which region is mine?¶

    The setup tool finds it from the town, street address or coordinates where the repeater will be, and the region map shows them all. Coordinates aren't sent to any search; a town or street address is looked up with the Government of Canada's place search (geo.ca).

    • Near a provincial border, pick the side it's really on. The yow region crosses the Ottawa River: an Ottawa repeater has yow and on, a Gatineau repeater yow and qc. A street address or coordinates settle it.
    • Towns in the same region share its code. Ottawa and Gatineau are both yow.
    • No zone for your area? MeshMapper, the community map that draws each region's area, doesn't cover everywhere. The region map then shows a MeshCore Canada starter region instead.
    • Write codes in lowercase. You can search for YTR, but on the air ytr and YTR are two different labels.

    The five labels¶

    From smallest to largest:

    Label Example What it covers
    Region ytr, yow, yhz One area, named after its airport code
    Province or territory on, qc, ns The province the repeater is in
    Ontario + Québec onqc Both provinces, as one shared mesh
    Canada can All of Canada. On every list, not used yet
    North America na Canada and the US. On every list, not used yet

    A Belleville repeater has ytr, on, onqc, can and na. A Halifax repeater has yhz, ns, can and na: onqc is only for Ontario and Québec.

    can and na are on every list so that Canada-wide or cross-border messages can work one day without anyone setting up their repeater again. Don't use them on your companion or channels yet: on their own, they don't make a message reach another province or country.

    Each label stands alone. The labels go from small to large, but they don't sit inside each other: having on doesn't mean having ytr. Each label has to be on the list by itself, which is why the setup tool puts every one that applies on every repeater.

    How a repeater decides¶

    When a message reaches a repeater, the repeater compares its label with its list:

    • The label is on its list: it passes the message on.
    • The label isn't on its list: it doesn't.
    • No label at all: the message counts as *, the star that stands for "no label". It's passed on only if * is allowed, which city repeaters do and edge repeaters don't. Older apps send messages with no label, and so does a companion with no default scope.
    • Too many hops: after 16 hops it stops, whatever its label.

    A Belleville city repeater's list is *, ytr, on, onqc, can and na. So:

    Message Label Belleville city repeater
    Belleville test channel ytr Passes it on
    First message to a friend in Montréal onqc Passes it on
    Someone on an older app none Passes it on
    Kingston channel ygk Doesn't pass it on
    Québec-wide channel qc Doesn't pass it on

    The repeater still hears every message: its list only decides which ones it passes on. Your companion receives everything that reaches it.

    A few details catch people out:

    • Spelling must be exact. ytr, YTR and quinte are three different labels.
    • Only messages that spread out are checked. Channel messages, and the first direct message to a contact, spread out through every repeater that will pass them on. This is called a flood. Once a direct message has a known path, it follows that path and labels no longer matter.
    • A repeater with no regions set passes on only messages with no label. A labelled message only travels once the repeaters along the way have its label.
    • A label isn't a lock or a fence. Anyone can put any label on a message, and it doesn't check where you are. It only tells repeaters whether to pass the message on.

    Who passes on what¶

    Here's a stretch of mesh from Belleville to Kingston, with an edge repeater in between:

    • Belleville city repeaters have *, ytr, on, onqc, can and na.
    • The edge repeater has ytr, ygk, on, onqc, can and na, but not *.
    • Kingston city repeaters have *, ygk, on, onqc, can and na.

    Does each repeater pass the message on?

    • No label, sent in Belleville: Belleville passes it on. The edge repeater doesn't, so Kingston isn't reached. It stays around Belleville.
    • ytr, a Belleville channel: Belleville and the edge repeater pass it on. Kingston's repeaters don't, so it stops there.
    • ygk, a Kingston channel: the edge repeater and Kingston pass it on. Belleville doesn't, so it stays around Kingston.
    • on, an Ontario channel: all three pass it on, so it goes across Ontario.
    • onqc, a companion's default: all three pass it on, so it goes across Ontario and Québec.

    This shows which repeaters pass a message on, not radio coverage. Anyone in range of a repeater still hears what it sends, and real meshes have more paths than this.

    Why airport codes?¶

    • MeshMapper already uses them, for example ytr.meshmapper.net.
    • MeshCore already uses them elsewhere, such as the location code every observer reports. A second set of names just for regions would only add confusion.
    • There are no accents or capital letters to get wrong. montréal, Montreal and montreal would be three different labels.
    • They're easy to split later. If a town wants its own area one day, it can use its own airport code and still keep its province and onqc.

    The code names the area, not the airport. The codes come from IATA, the airline industry group that assigns airport codes, which is why the map calls these IATA regions.

    Reading a region def line¶

    You don't need this to set up a repeater: the tool gives you the exact line. It helps when you want to read one.

    region def ytr|* on|* onqc|* can|* na

    region def builds the repeater's list in one line. It adds each name in turn, and |* means "go back to the top" before the next one, so the names end up side by side, not inside each other. Send region afterwards to see the list:

    *^ F
 ytr F
 on F
 onqc F
 can F
 na F
    • F means it passes those messages on.
    • The * line is for messages with no label. It has F on a city repeater and no F on an edge repeater.
    • ^ marks its home region. On a new repeater it sits on *. You can ignore it.

    region def only adds names. It never removes the ones already there, so a repeater that's already running has its old regions cleared first: see Clearing old regions. Firmware older than 1.16 builds the same list one name per line: see Older firmware.

    How messages find their way between cities¶

    You don't need to hear every repeater along the way to message someone in another city. The first direct message to a contact, before a path is known, spreads out through the mesh with your default scope. The reply that tells your companion the path comes back with your contact's default scope. After that, messages follow the saved path and labels no longer matter.

    So both default scopes have to be labels the repeaters in between pass on. Repeaters set up with the tool in Ontario and Québec all have onqc, so it works between Belleville and Montréal in both directions. A region label like ytr stops at the first repeater without it, just outside Quinte West, and a message with no label stops at the first edge repeater. That's why companions set a default scope: see Companion scopes.

    You still need your contact's details and working radio links between you. The MeshCore FAQ explains how paths are found.

    What each setting does¶

    The setup tool explains every command in its list in one sentence. These are the same explanations, all in one place. The examples are for a repeater in Belleville, whose region is ytr (Quinte West). Your list uses your own town, region and name.

    You paste the commands one at a time into the flasher's Console, or send them from the repeater's Command Line in the MeshCore app. Most answer OK. Lines with words between < and > are ones you type yourself: the tool never asks for your passwords, so it can't copy them for you.

    Basics¶

    set radio 910.525,62.5,7,5
    Use the Canada radio settings: 910.525 MHz, 62.5 kHz, spreading factor 7, coding rate 5. Every device nearby has to match.
    set radio 910.425,62.5,7,5

    Use the BC Mesh radio settings: 910.425 MHz, 62.5 kHz, spreading factor 7, coding rate 5. Every device nearby has to match.

    The tool asks which settings to use only where a local group, such as BC Mesh in British Columbia, uses its own.

    When you send the commands from your phone, the radio line is left out: changing the radio settings from your phone would cut off the connection you're using.

    set name Belleville_R1

    The name other people see.

    Keep it to about 30 letters (accented letters count as two). Spaces are fine, but not [ ] \ : , ? *: the repeater won't accept them.

    set lat 44.1628
    Show it on maps near Belleville (not its exact spot). This line sets the latitude.
    set lon -77.3832

    This line sets the longitude, so maps show it near Belleville.

    Untick the map option in the tool and both lines are left out.

    set lat 44.1628, when you typed a street address or coordinates
    Show it on maps at the spot you entered. This line sets the latitude.
    set lon -77.3832, when you typed a street address or coordinates
    This line sets the longitude, so maps show it at the spot you entered.
    password <your new admin password>
    Set the admin password you'll use to manage it from the app. Keep it in your password manager.
    password <your new admin password>, on a repeater that's already running
    Change the admin password, or skip this line to keep the current one. Keep it in your password manager.

    MeshCore Canada settings¶

    The tool gives these four to every repeater, so repeaters across the mesh behave the same way. An announcement (advert) is how a device tells others it exists. These save by themselves.

    set path.hash.mode 2
    Use longer repeater IDs (3 bytes) in the list of repeaters a message went through, so fewer repeaters share an ID.
    set advert.interval 240
    Announce itself to repeaters in direct range every 4 hours.
    set flood.advert.interval 47
    Announce itself across the wider mesh every 47 hours.
    set flood.max 16
    Never pass on a message after 16 hops (each repeater that passes it on is one hop).

    Scopes¶

    The scope lines for a Belleville city repeater on firmware 1.16 or newer:

    region def ytr|* on|* onqc|* can|* na
    Pass on messages labelled for Quinte West (ytr), Ontario (on), Ontario + Québec (onqc), Canada (can) and North America (na).
    region allowf *
    Also pass on messages with no label, as a city repeater does.
    region denyf *

    Don't pass on messages with no label, so they stay in their own region. That's what makes it an edge repeater.

    An edge repeater gets this line instead of region allowf *.

    region default ytr
    Label this repeater's own announcements with ytr.
    region save
    Save the region settings.

    Back up a running repeater¶

    A repeater that's already running has an identity: its private key. Other devices know it by that key, and it can't be recreated if it's lost. Before you flash it, back it up:

    1. Write down its name and admin password.
    2. To save its private key, in the MeshCore app open the repeater from Contacts, log in as admin, tap Command Line and send get prv.key. Or connect it by USB, open the Console in the MeshCore flasher and send the same command. Keep the reply in your password manager and never paste it anywhere shared.
    3. Never erase it in the flasher.

    If flashing wipes it anyway, for example when you switch to the observer firmware, put the key back. Right after flashing, while it's still plugged in, send set prv.key <saved key> in the flasher's Console, then reboot. Put the key you saved in place of <saved key>. You can also do it later from the app's Command Line if you can log in as admin; if you can't, plug it in by USB and use the flasher's Console. Both commands are in the MeshCore command reference.

    Never share the private key: anyone who has it can pose as your repeater.

    Clearing old regions¶

    A repeater that's already running may still have regions from an older setup. region def doesn't remove them, so the tool's list clears them first.

    region
    Show the regions it has now. Paste the reply into the setup tool to clear the old ones.
    region remove ott
    Remove the old region ott.
    region home ytr

    Make ytr its home region, since its old home region is being removed.

    This line only appears when the ^ home marker was on one of the old regions.

    The tool removes the most indented names first and never removes *. If region remove answers Err - not empty, another name is still indented under it: remove that one first, then try again. Err - not found means it's already gone, which is fine.

    From the app, a long reply can be cut short. If the list looks cut off, or the repeater has many regions, run region over USB instead, in the flasher's Console.

    Older firmware¶

    Firmware 1.16 and newer builds the whole list with one region def line. Older versions add one name per line, so these lines repeat for each label.

    Firmware 1.15:

    region put ytr
    Pass on messages labelled for Quinte West (ytr).

    Firmware 1.14:

    region put ytr
    Add Quinte West (ytr) to its list of regions. It doesn't pass those messages on until its region allowf line.
    region allowf ytr
    Pass on messages labelled for Quinte West (ytr).

    Firmware 1.14 has no region default, so the repeater's own announcements carry no label. Updating is worth it. Below 1.14, region settings don't work: update the firmware first.

    Repeater observer¶

    These lines are only for a repeater observer.

    set mqtt.iata YTR

    Tell live.meshcore.ca which region it reports from.

    It's the region code, in capitals.

    set wifi.powersave none
    Keep Wi-Fi awake so reports aren't delayed.
    set mqtt1.preset meshcore-ca-1
    Report to the main MeshCore Canada server. This replaces whatever is in MQTT slot 1, the first of its saved server connections.
    set mqtt2.preset meshcore-ca-2

    Report to the backup MeshCore Canada server too. This replaces whatever is in MQTT slot 2, the second of its saved server connections.

    If this board already reports to another network in those slots, use the observer flasher's Configure via USB to put MeshCore Canada in free slots (3 to 6) instead.

    set mqtt.status on
    Send a short status report when it connects and every few minutes, so live.meshcore.ca shows it as online.
    set mqtt.packets on
    Report the packets (radio messages) it hears. This is the main thing it sends to the servers.
    set mqtt.raw off
    Don't also send a raw copy of each packet. The servers don't need it.
    set mqtt.rx on
    Include the packets it receives over the radio.
    set mqtt.tx advert
    Of the packets it sends itself, report only its own announcements.
    set bridge.enabled on
    Turn on sending over Wi-Fi. This is the main switch for everything it reports.
    set repeat on
    Also pass messages on, as a full repeater.
    set repeat off
    Listen only: never pass messages on.
    advert
    Announce itself now, so nearby devices see it straight away.
    set wifi.ssid <your Wi-Fi name>
    Your 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi network. Type its name exactly, without quotes.
    set wifi.pwd <your Wi-Fi password>
    Your Wi-Fi password. Never paste this anywhere shared.

    Finish¶

    reboot
    Restart so every setting takes effect.

    After it restarts, the tool lists a few checks, such as ver and region. They only show its settings. The one exception is clock sync, which sets its clock from your phone: after a restart its clock is often wrong, and phones may ignore its announcements until it's set. Send it from the MeshCore app. It doesn't work from the Console: there the repeater only answers that its clock can't go backwards.

    Loop detection¶

    The tool doesn't change this setting. Firmware 1.14 and newer can drop packets that keep coming back through the same repeater, which can calm a flood of repeated packets. Change it only with your local operators: write down the current value with get loop.detect, set it with, for example, set loop.detect moderate, and put the old value back if messages start going missing. See the MeshCore command reference.

    City or edge repeater¶

    Edge repeaters regularly hear repeaters in another region, for example one between Belleville and Kingston. City is every other repeater, even on the outskirts. Edge repeaters stop messages with no label from spilling into the next region.

    The difference is what they do with messages that have no label:

    • A city repeater passes them on (region allowf *), so people nearby who haven't set a scope are still heard.
    • An edge repeater doesn't (region denyf *). It also has the codes of the neighbouring regions it hears, so labelled messages for either side get through.

    Belleville, city repeater:

    region def ytr|* on|* onqc|* can|* na
region allowf *
region default ytr

    Belleville, edge repeater that also hears Kingston (ygk):

    region def ytr|* ygk|* on|* onqc|* can|* na
region denyf *
region default ytr

    Why it matters¶

    Without edge repeaters, every message with no label would spread from town to town across the whole mesh, crowding the radio channel everywhere. Edge repeaters keep that local chatter in its own region, while labelled messages still travel as far as their label says.

    Is mine an edge repeater?¶

    • Being near a boundary isn't enough. What counts is which repeaters it regularly hears.
    • Check its neighbours on MeshMapper. Click your repeater to see the repeaters it hears, with how often and how recently each link was seen. Several neighbours in another region, heard often and recently, make it an edge repeater. A single link seen a few times, or not for weeks, doesn't count.
    • Towns that share a code are one region. Ottawa and Gatineau are both yow, so a repeater between them is a city repeater.
    • Think twice if it's the only repeater around. An edge repeater doesn't pass on messages from people nearby who haven't set a scope.
    • Every repeater linking the same two regions should be an edge repeater. Otherwise messages with no label go around through the one that isn't.

    An edge repeater lists the neighbouring regions it regularly hears. It will also carry messages labelled for these. They're optional: without them, it still passes on on, onqc and the other wider labels.

    Labels shared across the border¶

    Some repeaters link with a network in the United States. For those, the tool offers extra labels:

    • onny, a shared Ontario–New York label, for a city or edge repeater in Ontario.
    • qcny, a shared Québec–New York label, for a city or edge repeater in Québec.
    • Nearby US networks, for edge repeaters: Western New York, Pennsylvania and Ohio from Ontario; Washington and Oregon from British Columbia.
    • Another scope we've agreed with a network across the border, for any repeater in any province. Type the scope name exactly as you both agreed it (lowercase letters, numbers and dashes).

    Only tick one if this repeater links with that network and its operators have agreed. Not sure? Leave them off.

    Using a cross-border scope that isn't listed? Share it with us so it can be added to the tool.

    Still too noisy?¶

    If messages with no label are still too noisy inside a region, repeaters can also limit how far they travel with set flood.max.unscoped 3. Labelled messages keep the 16-hop limit. The tool doesn't add this line: only use it if your local operators agree.

    Companion scopes¶

    Your companion puts a scope on what you send. Each channel can have its own, and everything else uses your default scope.

    Set your default scope now

    Repeaters set up with the meshcore.ca setup tool pass messages on by scope. Edge repeaters don't pass on messages that have no scope, so without a default scope your messages may not reach beyond your own area.

    • Ontario or Québec: use onqc.
    • Elsewhere: use your province's code, such as ns or bc, unless your community has agreed on something else.

    In the MeshCore app, version 1.43 or newer, it's under Settings → Network Settings → Default Region Scope. The companion guide shows each step with screenshots.

    Why onqc? You can't give a single direct message its own scope. The first one to a contact uses your default scope, and the reply that sets up the path uses your contact's (see How messages find their way between cities). onqc is passed on across both provinces, so it works between any two cities there. Elsewhere there's no label shared between provinces, so your province's code is the widest one in use. Don't use can or na yet.

    Scope a channel¶

    A channel with no scope of its own uses your default scope. To give a channel its own, pick its label from this table, then follow the steps below.

    Channel Scope
    Public onqc in Ontario and Québec; your province's code elsewhere
    Test channels, such as #testing Your region, such as ytr
    Bot channels, such as #bots Your region
    Your own channels Your region, your province or onqc, as the people using it agree

    Do this for each channel. The screenshots set #testing to yow; use your own label instead.

    1. Tap Channels in the bottom menu bar and open the channel.

    2. Tap ⋮ in the top right and choose Set Region Scope.

    3. If your label isn't listed, tap +, type it (for example yow) and tap the checkmark.

    4. If you added one, you'll see Region has been added! Tap your label to select it.

    5. The header now shows Region: yow. Only repeaters with that label on their region list pass this channel's messages on.

    Scoped messages are a little shorter: the app allows fewer characters per message. On Public, the limit dropped from 137 to 127 characters in our testing.

    Bots and MeshMapper¶

    Bots and MeshMapper use their region's label, such as ytr, so their messages stay in their own area.

    • Bots: on the bot's companion, set Default Region Scope to your region code, the same way as above. Set the channels it posts in to your region too.
    • MeshMapper: use your region code as the scope for its messages.

    A labelled message only travels through repeaters that have its label, so check that the repeaters around you have been set up with it.

    Starter regions¶

    Where MeshMapper has no zone yet, MeshCore Canada fills the gap with a starter region. Starter regions use real airport codes and work like any other region, but they're not MeshMapper zones, and they don't claim radio coverage. The map draws them with dashed gold outlines. Check with local operators before relying on one.

    Area Region Code from
    Prince Edward Island yyg Charlottetown
    Newfoundland yyt St. John's
    Labrador yyr Happy Valley–Goose Bay
    Yukon yxy Whitehorse
    Northwest Territories yzf Yellowknife
    Nunavut yfb Iqaluit

    In the other provinces, these hubs fill the remaining gaps:

    Land that no region covers goes to the closest regional centre in its province: a MeshMapper region or one of the hubs above.

    A repeater in Charlottetown has yyg, pe, can and na; one in Whitehorse has yxy, yt, can and na. Neither uses onqc. The setup tool gives the commands.

    Planning extensions¶

    A gap right next to a MeshMapper region can use that region's code as a planning extension. For example, the Wingham-area point 43.8678, -81.2619 is in ykf, not Sudbury's ysb. Goderich is in yxu, and Kincardine in ylk.

    Extensions are separate dashed areas on the map. They don't change MeshMapper's zones, and the map says which kind of area your point is in. Like starter regions, they need local review and don't show measured radio coverage.

    Starter regions and extensions are broad starting points, not a promise that distant towns can hear each other. MeshMapper's zones always come first: ask in MeshMapper to add or change a zone. To suggest a change to a starter region or an extension, send a community proposal.

    The outlines follow Statistics Canada's province and territory boundaries. Labrador is census divisions 10 and 11; Newfoundland is the rest of the province.
